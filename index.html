The Bushfire Crisis In Australia

Bushfires in Australia have always been a serious issue, the so called "Lucky Country" has been victim to many of the worst fire periods that any country on earth has experienced, earning names such as the Black Summer in 2019, and being known as the Country on Fire at times. But what is the true reason behind why we are always on fire? Is there an answer? Is there a comparison to the rest of the globe? And finally, are we getting better?

The Australian Impact & The Black Summer

Just how intense has the previous fire seasons been in Australia - and how bad was the Black Summer.

This map indicates the total burns per state (not yet normalised - still to do) across the last 5 fire seasons (summers). The information shows where in Australia experiences the most burns, noticeably being the more northern states closer to the equator, NT and QLD. The peak of which being in 2016-17 for Queensland and 2019-20, The Black Summer, for the NT. There is a notable occurrence in 2020-21 in the ACT where a total of 0 burns where achieved, this drop off helps to identify the management taken after it achieved its personal peak in the Black Summer.

The line chart represents the total area burnt throughout each year across the entirety of Australia. There is a clear drop off from 2019 (approx 36M) to 2020 (approx 19M), eluding to the recovery techniques taken after such a devastating season.

Fire Area Types

Are there certain types of forets or area that are more likely to have a fire?

The stacked bar chart represents how many fires occur in each type of forest/landtype in Australia per year. Highlighting that since roughly 1996 there has been a clear lead by arid rangelands and tropical woodlands comparred to actual temperate forest. Matching the terrain seen in rural areas such as the NT.

This bar chart identifies the amount of times a region has been burnt. The data shows that most forests in Australia have only been burnt once, but the amount for multiple times is by no means 0.
